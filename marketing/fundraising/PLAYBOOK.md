# HireAlpha fundraising playbook

Research checked September 6, 2026. Founder says the product just launched yesterday; exact launch date, revenue, team background, incorporation, burn, and raise amount remain unconfirmed. Recommendations below are strategic judgments, not predictions of funding. Application dates can change; verify the linked page before submission.

## The strategy

Apply to investors who can underwrite founders before mature traction, while spending most of your time earning repeat customer use. A fresh launch does not prevent fundraising, but it cannot establish retention or product-market fit. My suggested allocation for the next month: 75% customers/product, 25% fundraising. Adjust if runway is urgent.

Positioning draft grounded in `marketing/launch-kit/00-facts.md`:

> HireAlpha is a proactive personal assistant in your texts. It remembers context, sends useful briefs, and helps manage everyday tasks through connected tools, asking before sending or spending. The personal assistant has launched; work and founder assistants are planned expansions.

This is a description from project documentation, not a fresh production audit. Recheck claims in a real demo before sending. Avoid opening with three different products for three different audiences. Choose the first customer segment from actual launch conversations; busy independent professionals is a hypothesis to test, not a validated segment.

## What to do correctly

1. **Sell one recurring job.** Interview users about the last real occasion they needed it, their current workaround, and what they already pay. Pick the task that gets users to return. Demonstrate the input, result, and approval step in 60–90 seconds.
2. **Show founder advantage.** Explain why you understand this customer, what you personally built, how you reached early users, and what changed after observing them. Founder biography and accomplishments must be supplied, not invented.
3. **Report honest cohorts.** Show signups, connected accounts, activated users, trials, actual paid users, cancellations, and eligible retention separately. With one day of history, day-7/day-30 retention is “not yet observable,” not zero and not a forecast.
4. **Prove useful outcomes.** Track tasks successfully completed and accepted briefs, not just number of messages. A proactive system can create message volume without delivering value. Record failures, user corrections, human intervention, and repeat usage by task.
5. **Understand cost per retained customer.** Include model calls, retries, messaging infrastructure, tools, hosting, payment costs, and support. Measure heavy users as well as averages. Calculate contribution at the actual promotional price separately from the regular price.
6. **Explain how you win.** iMessage is an entry point, not enough evidence of a durable advantage. Investigate whether trusted workflows, accumulated context, execution quality, or a repeatable distribution channel create customer preference. A referral incentive alone is not a network effect.
7. **Raise against milestones.** Build a monthly cash model and decide what capital buys: reliable delivery, repeat use in a specific segment, paid conversion, and a tested acquisition channel. Do not pick a round size because it sounds impressive.
8. **Run a focused process.** Start with five suitable prospects, learn from their objections, then approach the next five. Ask what evidence would change their decision and who actually decides. Track each next action.
9. **Check investors too.** Ask portfolio founders about responsiveness, behavior when a company struggled, follow-on expectations, and how introductions actually worked. Ask about close portfolio conflicts before sharing sensitive details.

## What to avoid

- Calling free trials or discounted subscriptions full-price MRR; multiplying a launch-day spike into an annual revenue claim; describing roadmap features as shipped.
- Claiming “everyone with an iPhone” as the initial market. Model a reachable customer group, adoption assumptions, annual revenue per customer, and expansion separately. For scale intuition only: $100M annual subscription revenue at $19/month requires about 438,600 full-price subscribers before discounts and churn. That is arithmetic, not a market forecast.
- Pretending “we use AI,” separate agent personalities, or having an iMessage number proves defensibility.
- Hiding dependence on messaging providers, delivery reliability, model providers, or manual work. Prepare the dependency map, documented operating constraints, failure metrics, and a fallback plan; label unshipped fallbacks as planned.
- Mass emailing hundreds of investors, fabricated personalization, invented competing offers, fake deadlines, or asking an acquaintance for twenty introductions at once.
- Paying someone who guarantees funding or giving substantial equity merely for introductions. Have counsel review any fundraising intermediary arrangement before agreeing.
- Negotiating only headline valuation. Model cumulative SAFE dilution, discounts, MFN clauses, pro-rata rights, option pools, governance, and side letters.
- Publicly posting investment terms before determining the permitted offering route with counsel. In the US, Rule 506(b) prohibits general solicitation; 506(c) permits it subject to accredited-purchaser and verification requirements. A warm intro is not by itself proof of compliance. [SEC guidance](https://www.sec.gov/resources-small-businesses/capital-raising-building-blocks/general-solicitation), [exemptions](https://www.sec.gov/about/exempt-offerings).
- Treating an interested email, a verbal commitment, or an unsigned term sheet as cash received. Track interested, committed, signed, and funded separately.

## What goes in the deck and data room

Make a concise 8–10-slide deck: customer/problem; working solution; why now; initial segment; observed traction; distribution experiments; alternatives and advantage; business model/costs; founder advantage; capital needed and milestones. Show roadmap as roadmap. Avoid unsupported market-size numbers and invented projections presented as facts.

Prepare a lightweight private data room: company formation and ownership documents if they exist; founder/contractor IP assignments; cap table and prior financing; monthly cash plan; dated metric definitions and cohort table; consented/redacted demo; architecture/dependency summary; relevant privacy and security materials. Share deeper material progressively. Do not send raw user messages, tokens, private bank data, or customer email exports.

Know these financing concepts before signing: a SAFE gives rights to future equity; a convertible note is debt; a priced equity round sets a share price. Standard post-money SAFEs make dilution easier to estimate. Illustration only: $500K at a $5M post-money cap is approximately 10% before subsequent new-money/option-pool dilution, assuming conversion at the cap. Different conversion conditions and other instruments change the result. Use counsel and a complete cap-table model; this is not a recommended valuation. [YC SAFE resources](https://www.ycombinator.com/safe).

Illustrative cash model only: $20K monthly net burn × 18 months + $60K buffer − $40K existing cash = $380K needed. Replace every input. Funding should take you to meaningful evidence with time left to raise again; fundraising itself is not the operating milestone.

## First 30 days

The quantities here are suggested work targets, not investor requirements or promises of traction.

| When | Customer/product work | Fundraising work |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–3 | Personally observe 5–10 onboarding sessions; choose a first recurring task; instrument success/failure and cohort eligibility. | Supply founder facts, company status, launch counts and budget; record a real demo; draft deck. |
| Days 4–7 | Reach 15–20 customer conversations; fix the leading activation failure. | Complete PearX, YC, Speedrun, Hustle and eligible Precursor applications; ask five genuine contacts for one relevant intro each. |
| Days 8–14 | Measure the first eligible day-7 cohorts and trial-to-paid conversion. Inspect canceled and inactive users. | Hold initial meetings; log objections; revise positioning using evidence, not invented urgency. |
| Days 15–21 | Test one low-cost acquisition channel; measure activated and retained users per dollar and founder hour. | Send relevant progress updates to interested investors; approach the next small group if the story improved. |
| Days 22–30 | Analyze repeat use, costs, support burden and full-price intent. Day-30 retention may still not be mature. | Decide whether to run a concentrated raise, continue relationship-building, or spend another month improving retention. |

Definitions: activation = completion of a chosen useful task (define before measuring); day-7 retention = eligible activated users completing a useful activity in the defined day-7 window / all activated users eligible for that window; trial-to-paid = trials that actually paid / trials whose conversion window elapsed. Show raw counts with percentages, acquisition channel, discounts, refunds, and observation window. Track user-initiated and proactive interactions separately.
